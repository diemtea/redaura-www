The gap

Trust without reach

A drug-checking team finds a mislabelled sample at a festival at 1am. They have the result, the equipment, and twenty years of trust with the people at that festival. Their only way to spread the warning is a whiteboard outside the tent and a post that reaches whoever is already following them.

Reach without speed

National monitoring systems have the reach, but not this mandate. They aggregate, they track trends, they issue guidance over weeks. They were never built to put a warning on thirty thousand phones tonight.

How it works

01

Only vetted organizations publish

Accreditation happens before an account exists. A volunteer at the testing point drafts the alert; a designated lead releases it. Two people, every time.

02

Structured text, universally accessible

Alerts are built from fixed fields—never prose or static graphics. Every advisory works natively with screen readers, braille displays, and 20+ languages, with severity carried by explicit text tokens, never color alone.

03

Built for dead zones.

Festival cell networks saturate exactly when alerts matter most. The app opens instantly with last-known alerts, shows when it last synced, and catches up on the thinnest sliver of signal. Alerts arrive inside the push itself — readable even when nothing else loads.

04

Nobody is tracked

Matching happens on the device — the phone works out whether it's inside an alert area. Nothing is transmitted, nothing is logged, nothing can be subpoenaed. That's the architecture, not a setting.

05

The data goes both ways

Because alerts are structured, the same publication that reaches phones tonight can reach national monitoring tonight, in a format their systems already read.

The demo app — live prototype

One app.

Users download a single global client; the alerts on it belong to the organizations that publish them — their name, their logo, their lab reference. The trust built on the ground travels with the warning.

Tap an alert to open it. A push lands a few seconds later, or replay it under the phone.

Open by design

Alerts are published in an open, documented format — any org, lab, or national early-warning system can publish and consume them. Zero lock-in, fully mappable to the CAP emergency-alert standard.

Yours forever

Export all alert data, schemas, and history at any time. No proprietary formats or locked databases.

Interoperable

Connects directly to existing early-warning systems and public safety infrastructure.

Mission-proof

The open specification outlives any single company or organization. Harm reduction infrastructure stays public.
